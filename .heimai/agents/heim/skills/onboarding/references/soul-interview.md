# The soul interview

An optional head start for a person's soul files. By default none is needed: every wrap compares the session with the soul files and updates them from what happened. The interview is for someone who wants their agents calibrated from day one, or whose files have drifted. Good questions beat a blank page, because the more experienced the person, the less visible their own way of working is to them. Where the files live and what goes in each is the [soul-files skill](../../soul-files/SKILL.md); this page is only how to ask.

## Where the answers go

| What | File | Where |
|---|---|---|
| How agents should work with the person everywhere | `soul.md` | the person's own Heim |
| Who they are: values, drivers, ways of thinking | `identity.md` | the person's own Heim |
| Where things stand now: role, people, dependencies | `heartbeat.md` | the person's own Heim |
| How agents should work with them in this Heim only | `soul.md` | the Heim's `users/<id>/` |

Never finances, health, family or relationships, which belong to the person's own agent. Never the `private/` part. An agent's own method never goes into a soul file.

## Create: the interview, about twenty minutes

If the person already has soul files, switch to *review*. Never overwrite without a yes.

Frame it: "This takes about twenty minutes: five areas, about how you actually work, not the official version. After each I say back what I heard and you correct it. Nothing is saved until you have approved all five."

One question per message. After each area, summarize in your own words and ask: "Does that match how it really works? Anything to correct?" Write nothing until the end.

1. **Rhythm.** How the days and weeks actually run: the morning before anything goes wrong, when hard thinking works best and when talking does, the days that always go a certain way, when energy drops and what helps, what separates a good week from a bad one. → `soul.md` (when and how to bring things), `heartbeat.md`.
2. **Decisions.** The decisions they make most often, which are easy and which drain them, what they need to know before deciding, what would have to be true to hand a decision over. → `soul.md` (when to ask, when to act, how much to propose unasked), `identity.md`.
3. **Dependencies and trust.** Who they regularly need things from, the one person or system that breaks the day when it breaks, the outside triggers that reliably affect the work, their rule for secrets. → `heartbeat.md`, `soul.md`.
4. **Standards.** What they could explain to a smart new colleague that is in no document, the mistakes others make that they never would, the standards they hold but never wrote down, the context agents keep lacking. → `soul.md`, `identity.md`, and the Heim's `soul.md` for what is specific to it.
5. **Friction.** What they explain over and over, the length and shape they want, words, symbols or tones they dislike, what a good and a bad end-of-session message looks like, where an agent would save them the most time. → `soul.md`.

Then draft every change, file by file and Heim by Heim, show it, and write on a yes. The person's own Heim is committed and pushed (or just saved, if it is a local folder); the Heim's file is committed.

## Synthesize: draft from history, then check

For a person with many sessions behind them. Gather their handovers, the corrections in their `soul.md` files across Heims, their own log and their objectives; group what you find into the five areas (direct corrections, repeated approvals, habits every wrap shows); cite at least one source with its date for every proposed line; then show the draft and ask what is wrong, missing or over-fitted (a one-time correction that became a rule). Ask only about the areas the history does not cover.

## Review: a drift check

When the files are older than about a month, or recent sessions contradict them: read both cabinets and the last thirty to sixty days of handovers and corrections, list each line the evidence contradicts and each recurring pattern not captured, quote the evidence, propose the change and ask. Apply what is confirmed; update and prune rather than append, and date the change.
