# Existing material: read first, ask only about the gaps

Read it when the person has anything already: a pitch, notes, a website, an old brief, research, a recording of them talking about it.

## The order

1. **Read all of it** before the first question. Sort what you find into the brief's topics (vision, positioning, business model, business customers, target users, concept, success, alternatives, constraints, and voice and look if they appear), each one **complete**, **partial** or **missing**.
2. **Show what you found, one topic at a time.** Quote or closely paraphrase the source so the person sees what you are working from, add your reading of it in one sentence, and ask whether it holds:

   > **Vision.** Your pitch says: "every dog walked, every day, by someone the owner trusts." My reading: it is about trust more than about walking. Does that still hold, or has it moved?

   Wait for the answer before the next topic. A correction is written in before you go on.
3. **Name the gaps** once the found topics are confirmed: what is missing, what is only touched on, what is implied but never said. Those are what the conversation is about from now on.
4. **Fill only the gaps,** with the ordinary [discovery](discovery.md). A confirmed topic takes thirty seconds ("We have your vision as …; anything to add?"), not ten minutes.

## Rules

- Never ask a question the material already answers. If the answer is there but old, ask whether it still holds, not what it is.
- Write down where each confirmed point came from (the document, or "the person, today"), in the document's sources or beside the point.
- No judgment about what exists or is missing.
- A recording is material like any other; private moments in it are left out.
