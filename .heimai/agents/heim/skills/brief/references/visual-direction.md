# Visual direction: `11-visual-direction.md` and the brand's direction

Read it when style, references, colour, typography or imagery come up. The aim is direction drawn from real examples the person has reacted to, so nobody later has to guess what "more modern" means.

## The organization's or the project's

The visual direction is usually the organization's: it goes in the brand folder (`visual-identity/visual-direction.md`), and `11` points there and adds only what is this project's own. If the brand already has a direction, read it first and talk only about what differs. Where the visual direction sits among the other sources of design is the guardrail [visual-authority](../../../guardrails/visual-authority.md).

## From real examples

1. **Ask for two to four sites, products or images** the person finds inspiring: a layout they like, a competitor, a style they admire. One example with one thing they like is enough to start. With none, offer two or three from their field and ask for reactions.
2. **Look at each one yourself**, in the browser, so you see what they see.
3. **Ask open first** ("What drew you to it?"), then about what is actually there: the layout, the navigation, how content is shown, the imagery, the type, how it moves and how fast it feels. No checklist read aloud.
4. **Write down the reaction and the reason.** Liked, disliked, or liked with a change; the reason matters more than the reaction.
5. **Say back the pattern you see** across the examples ("You seem to prefer few, large images and a lot of air; is that fair?"), and confirm the principles before writing them as agreed.

Taste's styles may serve as reference words in this conversation (`agents/taste/`), offered as examples, never as the answer.

## What to write

Per example, in `11` or in the brand's direction:

```markdown
### Example: <name or address>
- Liked: <the element>, because <why>
- Disliked: <the element>, because <why>
- Liked with a change: <the element>, but <the change>
```

Then the principles, from all the examples together: layout, content hierarchy, style, colour, typography, imagery, each as *do* and *don't*.

## Red flags

- **"Like that site"** without what in it: ask which part, and why.
- **Abstract words** ("clean", "modern", "premium"): ask for an example of each.
- **A finished design in the person's head:** write it as strong direction with its source, and leave the design itself to Freya.
